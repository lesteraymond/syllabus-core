Date: 2026-10-26.

- No migration ran, and no production data, curriculum record, syllabus or environment setting was touched. `server/.env` was last changed on 2026-10-25 and sets no stage, trim, title or `minItems` flag. Nothing was committed, pushed, merged or deployed.

## 1. Root causes

**Evaluation stopping.**
1. **Recoverable errors were counted as final failures.**
   - The invalid-answer stop counted every answer refused in whole or in part, retries included.
   - In E.5, 3 of 4 answers were refused in part, so the run stopped at its third, although 7 of its 8 relationships verified (4 of them after a correction).
   - The E.5 plan found 75–80% of answers invalid, so a 20% rule would have stopped every phase.
2. **`firstAttemptInvalid` was not a first-attempt rate.** `eval-alignment.cjs` counted every invalid answer under that name, retries included.
3. **Relationships never sent were scored as invalid.** `scoreEval` (`eval/metrics.js`) counted an item that a run never asked (because it stopped first) as an invalid answer, so a stop lowered the agreement figures.
4. **Phase 3's `--stop-invalid` mixed the unasked with the failed.** Its rate was taken over all of a syllabus's relationships, including those never sent.
5. **The repeated-error bound was wrong.** The E.5 plan gave at most 7 requests per batch. The bounded retries allow 1 + 2 × 4 = 9 for a batch of 4 (test #4).

**Empty answers.**

6. **The strict schema allowed an empty list.** `judgements` had no `minItems`, so `{"judgements":[]}` was a valid answer, and the shortest one. Two of the five first answers to a batch in E.4 and E.5 were that list (6 completion tokens each, status 200).

**Cost.**

7. **The guard charged 3–5 times the bill.** It charged every request at the highest list price and ignored the cache. In E.5 it charged $0.0112 where the provider reported $0.0022.

**Prompt examples.**

8. **The D1 example was an evaluation item.**
   - The Value-use test's example read: "Humility is integrated by a peer code review in which students acknowledge their own mistakes and accept constructive feedback". That describes the dev item `vu-prog1-humility-review`.
   - For `vu-prog1-name-only` the model repeated the example without its condition (E.5 section 7).
9. **The Value-map examples gave away expected mappings.**
   - They paired Respect with Community-orientedness and Honesty with Courage, and called Critical Thinking a competency.
   - Respect and Honesty are the supporting values of 3 Value-map items: `vm-prog1-respect-supported`, `vm-prog1-respect-no-evidence` and `vm-prog1-honesty-supported`.
10. **Stage B's example is the prog1 topic.** "A topic such as Java conditional statements belongs in an introductory programming course" shares its phrase with 10 item targets and 33 item sources. It is flagged, not changed (section 5).

**Structured fields.**

11. **A verdict could rest on a field the model did not assess.**
    - A Stage C or D answer could leave the field its verdict rests on as `not_assessed`, and the verdict still counted as verified.
    - In E.5, `af-syseng-irrelevant` left `teaching` unassessed, and its NOT_ALIGNED still verified.

## 2. Evaluation metrics and stops

**Counts** (`server/lib/syllabuscheck/eval/evalMetrics.js`, new). Every relationship is counted once, however often it is asked. One item asked in two runs counts as two relationships.

| Measure | What it counts |
|---|---|
| planned | the relationships the evaluation set out to ask |
| attempted | planned relationships sent at least once. A request the guard refused before sending is not a send |
| never attempted | planned and never sent. Never counted as evaluated, verified, agreement or unresolved |
| verified | relationships whose answer verified. A verified INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE answer counts, because the model answered |
| recovered | verified after their first answer was refused |
| unresolved | attempted and still not verified at the end |
| **final unresolved rate** | unresolved ÷ attempted, by distinct relationship. This decides completion |
| coverage | verified ÷ planned |
| first-attempt validation failure rate | of the relationships an answer came back for, the share whose first answer did not verify. A first send with no answer at all (a timeout) is counted apart (`noAnswerFirst`) |
| raw response errors | answers that failed validation in whole or in part (retries included), the refused judgements in them, and requests without an answer. An operational signal only |
| field coverage | the structured fields of the verified answers (section 7) |

**The E.5 run, counted again.** These are the same 4 model answers (`docs/stage-e5-reference-evidence.md`, section 6):

| Measure | E.5 reading | E.6 reading |
|---|---|---|
| raw | 3 of 4 answers invalid: the run stopped | 3 of 4 answers failed validation in whole or part (6 refused judgements): a warning |
| first attempt | – | 5 of 8 relationships refused (62.5%): `cp-bsie-real2`, and the 4 of the empty list |
| recovered | – | 4 |
| final unresolved | – | 1 of 8 (12.5%): `rr-syseng-unrelated`, whose correction the request cap left unsent |
| never attempted | – | 0 |
| verified | 7 of 8 | 7 of 8 (coverage 87.5%) |
| completion | stopped | **complete**: every planned relationship was attempted, within the 20% limit |

**Completion** (`completionOf`) has four states:
- `complete`: every planned relationship was attempted, and the final unresolved rate is at most `--max-unresolved` (0.2).
  - Only a complete run is `usableForAgreement`.
  - Even then, its agreement is with provisional expectations, not with faculty review.
- `incomplete`: some planned relationship was never attempted because the run stopped first, whatever the rate.
- `unresolved_above_limit`: every relationship was attempted, but too many remain unresolved.
- `not_started`: nothing was planned.

**Stops.** Every stop the guard had is kept.

| Kind | Condition | Changed? |
|---|---|---|
| hard | HTTP 401/402/403 stops at once. So does any other 4xx of a model request except 429 (the request format or routing is refused) | no |
| hard | the request cap (`--budget`), and the dollar cap. A request is sent only when the dollars charged, the reservations in flight and its own worst case fit under `--max-cost` | no (section 4) |
| hard | the 3rd rate limit, timeout, network or server error (`--max-transient`) | no |
| hard | a charge above a worst case, or more tokens than its bound | no |
| repeated errors | `--max-unproductive` answers in a row that verified no relationship (a provider or schema that keeps failing). Default 9: the most one batch of 4 can bring | new default |
| academic | between batches (in Phase 3, between syllabi): once `--min-sample` (8) distinct relationships were attempted, a final unresolved rate above `--max-unresolved` (0.2) | new |
| operational | answers that failed validation. A warning, once, at `--warn-invalid` (default 40% of `--budget`, at least 3: where the old stop was) | was a stop |

- **The invalid-answer stop is not removed.**
  - `--max-invalid N` still stops at N answers (test #1).
  - It is no longer the default, because an answer refused for one relationship and corrected by the next request is not a failed evaluation.
  - A provider that keeps failing is caught by the repeated-error stop. Too many unresolved relationships are caught by the academic stop.
- **Retries are unchanged.** Batches of 4; each relationship is sent at most 3 times, with corrections; verified judgements are kept and never asked again.

**Scoring.**
- `scoreEval(items, runs, { attempted })` leaves out of the score a relationship that a run never sent (`notAttempted`). One that was sent and never verified is still invalid. Without the option the score reads as it always did.
- `eval-alignment.cjs` reports `invalidAnswers` (formerly `firstAttemptInvalid`) beside the true first-attempt rate. Result files written before E.6 keep the old name.
- A structured field is compared with its expectation only when the answer assessed it. A field that is left out, `not_assessed`, outside its enum or in a contradictory answer counts as neither agreement nor disagreement.

**Phase 3** (`live-pipeline-eval.cjs`). The engine plans each stage from the answers before it, so a syllabus's relationships are known only once it is checked. `trackSyllabus` counts:
- every relationship the engine judged, and every id a request carried;
- from the engine's own stage counts, the relationships of a stage it planned but never named (a batch not reached after a stop). These are planned and never attempted.

A syllabus never checked is listed in `notStarted`, and it makes the run incomplete. `--stop-invalid` is still accepted, as the old name of `--max-unresolved`.

## 3. Empty answers and `minItems`

**What is known.**
- Both empty answers came back with status 200 from the same endpoint (InferenceNet), on mostly cached prompts (E.4 #8, E.5 #4).
- Other InferenceNet answers on cached prompts were complete. Five first answers cannot show whether the endpoint, the cache or the model is the cause.
- The server refuses an empty list for every relationship asked (`empty_answer`) and corrects it once. In E.5 the correction recovered 3 of 4.

**What was added, off by default.** `SYLLABUS_CHECK_SCHEMA_MIN_ITEMS=on` adds `minItems: 1` to the `judgements` array.
- It applies only to the strict json_schema request of a paid OpenRouter model, and only while a stage is on (`minItemsOn()` in `gemini.js`). It never applies with every stage off, or on the free models.
- The prompt version gains `-mi1` (for example `alignment-12d1-r7-mi1`), so answers made with and without it are never mixed in the cache.
- Nothing else in the schema changes. The strict objects keep `additionalProperties: false` and every property required (test #8).
- The scripts turn it on only with `--min-items`, and only in their own env object.

**What `minItems` cannot do.**
- One judgement is not one per id: `minItems: 1` still allows a single answer for 4 relationships.
- So the server checks every answer as before (test #9):
  - an empty list is `empty_answer`;
  - a missing id is `not_answered`;
  - an unknown or duplicate id is refused;
  - only the missing or refused relationships are asked again.
- An exact count (`minItems` = `maxItems` = the batch size) was considered and not used. It still says nothing about which ids are answered, and it would make the schema depend on the batch.

**Compatibility is not measured.** Only the provider can show whether OpenRouter passes `minItems` on to the endpoints that serve this model, and whether each one enforces it, ignores it or refuses the schema. Finding out needs a paid request, which is not approved.
- An endpoint that refuses the schema returns a 4xx. The guard treats that as a refused request format and stops at once.
- An endpoint that ignores it behaves as today.

Until this is measured, the flag stays off and the server-side validation is the protection. The revised Phase 2 keeps it off.

**Proposed probe** (needs approval; not run). One price lookup and at most 2 model requests, with every stage on, for the E.5 BSIE course:
```
node scripts/live-smoke-eval.cjs --live --confirm-paid --min-items --courses syseng --ledger docs/ai-eval-stage-e6-minitems-ledger.json --max-requests 3 --max-cost 0.05 --stage "E.6 minItems compatibility probe" --out docs/ai-eval-stage-e6-minitems.json
```
- It shows whether the schema is accepted and answered. It cannot measure the empty-answer rate (2 of 5 so far).
- The guard's worst case is about $0.021 per request (E.5: $0.0200–0.0208), so at most about $0.042 is charged. The bill would be much lower.

## 4. Cost accounting

**Reservation (unchanged).**
- A request's worst case is:
  - every request byte (+256 for the chat template) as an input token, at the highest input price any endpoint lists;
  - plus `max_tokens` (8192) at the highest output price;
  - plus the per-request fee.
- It is reserved before the request is sent, and never lowered while the request is in flight.
- A request is sent only when the dollars charged, the reservations in flight and its own worst case fit under `--max-cost`. Without current prices or `max_tokens`, nothing is sent.

**Settlement** (`--accounting reconcile`, the new default):

| When the request ends | Charged | `accounting` |
|---|---|---|
| a complete answer (status 200, read to the end, no error inside) whose reported cost is reliable | the reported cost | `reconciled` |
| a complete answer whose cost is missing or out of bounds | the larger of the reported cost and the reported tokens at the highest prices | `conservative` |
| a timeout, a network error, an abandoned or cut-off read, or no usage | the worst case | `worst_case` |
| refused (4xx) | what is reported, else nothing | `refused` |

- **"Reliable"** (`reliableCost()`) means both:
  - the prompt and completion tokens are reported and within the worst case's bounds;
  - the cost lies between those tokens at the lowest listed prices (cached tokens at the lowest cache-read price, with 2% tolerance) and at the highest.
- `--accounting conservative` keeps the E.5 rule for every request.
- **The ledger keeps both figures.**
  - `charged` follows the rule in force, and `conservative` is what the E.5 rule would have charged.
  - Each entry records its `accounting` and its `conservativeCharge`.
  - A ledger written before E.6 has no `conservative` figure, so it is read as equal to `charged`.
  - The key never reaches a ledger (test #7).
- **Every script works the same way.** `live-smoke-eval.cjs`, `eval-alignment.cjs` (Phase 2) and `live-pipeline-eval.cjs` (Phase 3) all send through this guard, and all take `--accounting`.
- **Dry runs.** The scripted provider bills at the cheaper of its two endpoints. With it:
  - a clean stage-d1 run was charged $0.0032 reconciled, against $0.0089 conservative;
  - a clean Phase 3 syllabus was charged $0.056 against $0.157.

  These figures follow the scripted prices, not a real bill.

## 5. Values evaluation (prompt r7)

**Value-use** (`stages.js` D1). The test now places a value statement in one of these kinds, and gives no worked example:

| `valueUse` | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `integrated` | a meaningful demonstrated integration: the value statement, or the lesson's activities or tasks, describe something students do or decide in this lesson that expresses the value |
| `opportunity` | a potential opportunity: the statement ties the value to an activity or task, but neither it nor the lesson says what students do or decide that expresses it |
| `name_only` | the value is merely named, whatever the activities would allow |
| `generic` | a sentence that would fit any lesson |
| `unrelated` | the value has nothing to do with what students do in this lesson |
| `insufficient` | the lesson text is too thin to tell |

The wording also says:
- That students *could* express a value does not show that they *do*. An activity never counts as integrating a value merely because it could, and the model never states that students do something the syllabus does not describe.
- A lesson with no activity or task cannot integrate a value through one.
- Only the definitions the request supplies are used, and no institutional requirement is invented.
- ALIGNED only when `valueUse` is `integrated` and `valueFit` is `fits` or `partly`. `carriedBy` is `none` unless the value is integrated.
- The draft proposes, for the author to confirm, how students could live the value in this lesson's own activity. It never presents that as already happening.

**Value-map** now has no examples:
- no supporting expression has a fixed core value;
- a mapping is made only from what the lesson says students do;
- an academic competency is never a core value.

**The verdict** (`verdict.js`; test #12). The official result is unchanged in every case.

## 6. Reference quotes

- **The validator is unchanged.**
  - A quote must appear in its own text exactly, apart from spacing. Capitals, spelling and punctuation count.
  - `quote_not_found`, `quote_wrong_side`, `quotes_swapped` and `quote_from_other_pair` are still refused.
- **New copy instruction** (`Q.exact`, only while a stage is on): "Copy each quote character for character from its text: the same capital and small letters, spelling and punctuation, with nothing corrected, re-capitalised, added or translated; a quote that differs from its text in anything but spacing is refused."
- **Test #15.**
  - The instruction is in every staged prompt and in no all-off prompt.
  - The quote "A History of Medieval Architecture", taken from an entry titled "A history of medieval architecture", is refused (`quote_not_found`, target side).
  - A difference in spacing only is accepted, and recorded as `whitespace`, not `exact`.
- **Untested live.** In E.5 a quote with a capitalised first word was refused, and its correction was never sent.

## 7. Unassessed structured fields

**The rule** (`aspects.js`, `verdict.js`):

| Type | Fields required | The base rule rests on | Base rule | Rules of the other fields |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Activity-fit | teaching, learnerSupport | teaching | ALN-05 | ALN-06 (learnerSupport) |
| Assessment-fit | measures, evidence, level | measures | ALN-07 | ALN-08 (evidence), ALN-09 (level) |
| Value-use | valueUse, carriedBy, valueFit | valueUse | VAL-03 | VAL-02 (valueFit); carriedBy has no rule |
| Value-map | coreValue, integration | coreValue and integration | VAL-05 | – |
| Ref-relevance | relevance, basis | relevance | REF-05 | basis has no rule |

- **A field is assessed** when it has a value of its enum other than `not_assessed`.
  - `not_applicable`, `insufficient` and `cannot_tell` are conclusions, so they count as assessed.
  - Any other field is `not_assessed`, `missing`, `malformed` or `contradicted`.
- **The base judgement stands when its own field was assessed**, even if another field was not.
  - If its own field was not assessed, the finding is NEEDS_REVIEW (`aspects_not_assessed`, severity MINOR). It never counts as a pass or a failure.
  - The explanation says so, for example: "The verdict rests on what the AI did not assess in this answer (the teaching method), so it is shown for review and not counted as a failure."
- **A field's own rule** is NOT_CHECKED (`aspect_not_assessed`) when that field was not assessed.
- **A contradictory answer** stays NEEDS_REVIEW (`contradictory_response`), as before.
- **Older answers.** An answer stored without any field record (from before Stage C) is read as before.

**Reporting.** Field coverage is reported apart from verification.
- Each lesson finding carries its own `aspectCoverage`.
- The verdict summarises, per type:
  - the relationships;
  - the base judgements verified, and how many of those stand on their own assessed field;
  - the answers stored without a field record;
  - each field, by state.
- The evaluation scripts add one clause to their summary line: "fields assessed *n* of *m* in the verified answers, *a* of *v* base judgements on their own assessed field".

**Offline before and after.** This compared 12 cases: three program syllabi, with every stage off and every stage on, each with clean answers and with answers that leave fields unassessed.
- The official result is identical in all 12.
- With every stage off, the prompts, schemas, prompt version, request count and verdict are identical.
- With every stage on, findings changed status only in the cases with unassessed fields:
  - ALN-05, ALN-07 and VAL-03 went from PASS to NEEDS_REVIEW;
  - ALN-06, ALN-08, ALN-09 and VAL-02 became NOT_CHECKED.

  - 3 transient errors;
  - 9 answers in a row that verify nothing;
  - a final unresolved rate above 0.2 once 8 relationships were attempted.
  - Invalid answers only warn, at 40% of `--budget`.

**What it measures** (each result file):
- **First-pass success**: the first-attempt validation failure rate, and the relationships verified on their first answer.
- **Final unresolved**: the count and the rate, and the completion state.
- **Agreement** with the provisional expectations, by type. These expectations are developer-written and unreviewed, so this is not faculty agreement.
- **Per-aspect verification**: the fields assessed per type, the base judgements that stand on their own assessed field, and field-level agreement where an expectation exists.
- **Cost**: the reconciled charge beside the conservative one, and the reported cost.
- **Items to read**: `vu-prog1-name-only` (the r7 value wording), and the Ref-relevance quote orientation and exact copies.

**What it cannot show.**
- Stability: there is one run per dataset.
- Held-out performance: these items are all on the dev split.
- Faculty agreement.
- The effect of `minItems`, which stays off.

- stage-a, stage-b, stage-b3 and stage-b4. Stage B's example should be replaced first, if that is approved.
- The general and alignment datasets.
- Phase 3. It should be re-planned from the costs and rates Phase 2 measures. With every stage on, Phase 3 is 423 model requests before retries, with a worst case of $0.0225–0.0234 per request.
