# Reviewer guide: Part A

Thank you for reviewing these cases. Your judgments will become the reference against which a syllabus-checking program is evaluated. They do not change any syllabus, grade or policy by themselves.

## What you receive

- **This guide.**
- **The curriculum and institutional references** (`02-curriculum-and-institutional-references`): the institutional texts held in the syllabus system, each with its source and status.
- **Your course's Part A form**, as a Word document you can fill in and as a PDF you can print, with a response sheet (CSV) if you prefer a spreadsheet. Each case shows one relationship between two outcomes, for example a course learning outcome and the program outcome it is mapped to, and asks for your judgment.
- **Part B, later.** After you return Part A, you will receive a separate Part B package. It asks you to rate changes proposed for some of the same outcomes.

## Before you start

- Please work on your own, and do not discuss the cases with other reviewers until the coordinator asks you to.
- Judge each relationship from the two texts and your professional knowledge of the course and program. You will not be shown how anyone else, or any software, judged these cases.
- **The cases are synthetic.** The course records, course descriptions and outcomes were written for this review to test the software. They were not written by faculty and are not taken from approved syllabi. The course codes and titles resemble the institution's programs; reference R-CODES shows which of them appear in the curricula transcribed in the system. The program outcomes in the cases are general wording, not your program's official outcomes. Judge every text as written.
- Some cases were written to be sound and some to be weak or mismatched. There is no expected proportion of either.
- If a case is unclear or badly posed, say so in the comments. That is useful information.
- A case's code tells you only its course and its place in your form.

## Status labels

Every text in the course context and the references carries one of these labels. **No text in this package has been confirmed by the institution.**

| Label | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Synthetic: written for this review | Written to test the software. Not written by faculty, not institutional text and not taken from an approved syllabus. Judge it as written. |
| Transcribed: copied into the system, not confirmed by the institution | Copied into the syllabus system from an institutional document (a form, a syllabus or a registrar curriculum image). The responsible office has not confirmed the copy as current and approved. |
| Website copy: pending institutional approval | Copied from the university website. The institution has not confirmed it as the approved wording for syllabus checking. |
| Not held in the system | The system holds no text for this. |

## Terms

- **CLO:** course learning outcome. **ILO:** intended learning outcome of a lesson or week, a smaller unit within the course. **PO:** program outcome. **EGA:** expected graduate attribute (the system holds four names).
- In a **CLO and PO** or **CLO and EGA** case, the CLO is judged against the PO or EGA it is mapped to.
- In an **ILO and CLO** case, the ILO is judged against the CLO it is meant to build toward.
- In a **list** case, the CLO is judged against a whole list. The question is whether it fits at least one item, and which.

## The questions on each form

**0. Within your expertise.** If a case is outside your expertise, tick **No** and leave the rest of that case blank. The coordinator will reassign it to another qualified reviewer. It is not counted as a judgment.

**1. Alignment.** Choose one:

- **Aligned:** in your judgment, the outcome supports the outcome it is mapped to.
- **Partially aligned:** there is a connection, but it is weak, indirect, incomplete in a way that matters, or at an inappropriate level.
- **Not aligned:** the outcome does not support the outcome it is mapped to.
- **Insufficient information to judge:** the texts given are not enough to decide. Say in question 2 what you would need.

These descriptions are deliberately general. If your answer depends on how alignment is defined, question 3 records how.

**2. Rationale.** Name the words or ideas in the texts that support your judgment, and anything missing.

**3. Reading you applied.** When an outcome develops only part of the outcome above it, colleagues read "aligned" in different ways: some count a part as enough, others require every part. The institution has not adopted a rule. Tick the reading you applied. Tick "The distinction did not affect my answer for this case" when your answer would be the same under either reading. Your answer lets each judgment be read correctly under whatever rule is adopted.

**4. Course fit.** Does the outcome itself belong in this course, at this year level? Choose **Unsure** if you have the information but cannot decide, and **Insufficient information to judge** if the materials do not give you enough. Question **4b** asks whether your course-fit answer affected your alignment answer. The institution has not decided whether course fit should affect the alignment verdict, so both answers are recorded.

**5. Recommendation.** What, if anything, should change: no change; revise the outcome's text; keep the text but map or link it to a different outcome; remove the outcome; refer the case for a policy decision; insufficient information to recommend; or something else. If you choose a different outcome, name it: quote enough of its words to identify it, from the outcomes listed under Course context or, for program outcomes, from your program's own outcomes. Write "none suitable" if none fits.

**6. Cognitive level** (optional). The revised Bloom's taxonomy level of each text (Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze, Evaluate, Create). Choose "Not applicable", for example for an attribute's name, or "Cannot tell".

**7. Confidence.**

- **High:** you would defend this judgment in a program review.
- **Medium:** reasonable colleagues might differ.
- **Low:** you are unsure or need more information.

**8. Comments.** Anything else, including problems with the case itself.

## Returning your form

- In Word, tick the boxes and type in the grey fields. On paper, print the PDF and write by hand. In the response sheet, write one row per case, using the words of the form's options.
- Tick one box where a question asks for one answer. If you change your mind on paper, cross out the old tick clearly.
- Write the reviewer code the coordinator gave you. Write your name only if the institution asks for it.
- Return the form to the coordinator by the agreed date. Once returned, Part A is final, and you will then receive Part B.

## Time

As a rough guide, allow three to five minutes for each case.
