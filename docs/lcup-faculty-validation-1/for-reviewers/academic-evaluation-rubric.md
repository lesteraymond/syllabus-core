# Academic evaluation rubric (provisional)

Package lcup-faculty-validation-1, built 2026-10-29. For faculty reviewers and the research team.

> Provisional draft, prepared on 2026-10-29 for the capstone research team and the faculty reviewers. It is not an LCUP policy, and it supplies no official LCUP definition. The faculty panel should review it, and the office the institution designates should approve or amend it, before it is treated as an institutional standard. It builds on the project's existing reviewer guide and on the seven academic relationships of the evaluation policy (academic-evaluation-v2-preview-1), and changes neither.

## Part 1. What you are judging

One academic relationship at a time, between two statements of one syllabus, using only the evidence shown with the case. You are not judging the whole syllabus, its format, its approval or its author. The syllabi were approved for use; that approval does not establish that every relationship in them is aligned.

## Part 2. The four judgments

Definitions as worded in the project's reviewer guide.

| Judgment | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `ALIGNED` | The supplied evidence supports this particular academic relationship. |
| `PARTIALLY_ALIGNED` | There is meaningful support, but a material gap or ambiguity remains. |
| `NOT_ALIGNED` | There is a substantiated mismatch in this particular relationship. |
| `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` | The supplied information cannot support a defensible judgment. |

How to choose:

1. Read the two statements in full, then the context: the whole week as printed, the course description and the CLOs.
2. Note what each statement is about (its subject matter) and, where it says so, what students are to do.
3. Ask the question of the relationship (Part 4) about this pair only.
4. Choose ALIGNED if the evidence supports the relationship and you see no material gap; PARTIALLY_ALIGNED if there is meaningful support but you can name a material gap or ambiguity; NOT_ALIGNED if you can point to the mismatch in the text; INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE if the statements, as written, cannot support a defensible judgment either way.
5. A gap is material if, in your professional judgment, it would change what is taught, practised or assessed. Say which gap you mean.
6. Write your justification and, if needed, a recommended correction (Part 5).

## Part 3. Academic alignment and structural compliance

This review concerns academic alignment: whether the meaning of two statements fits together. It does not concern structural compliance: whether the syllabus has the required parts in the required form. Structural compliance is checked separately, by rule-based checks that this review does not change.

| Structural compliance: not judged in this review | Academic alignment: judged in this review |
|---|---|
| Whether a field is present: an ILO carries a CLO tag, a lesson week has an ILO, a lesson names a core value | Whether the content of the two statements fits together |
| Format and numbering: codes, labels, week numbers, tags written in different styles | Whether what one statement requires is supported by the other |
| Counts and totals: number of CLOs, references, grading weights | Whether the scope and level of the two statements are consistent |
| Approval and sign-off of the document |  |

A structural problem does not by itself make a relationship misaligned, and a well-formed statement can still be misaligned. If you notice a structural problem, record it under Notes and base your judgment on meaning.

## Part 4. The seven relationships

### 4.1 Course Title ↔ Course Content

**Question.** Does this week's course content fall within the subject that the course title names, read together with the course description?

**Consider**

- the subject the title names, and the scope and level the course description gives it.
- every topic the week lists, not only its first line.
- the lesson outline, which shows where the week sits in the course.

| Judgment | When |
|---|---|
| `ALIGNED` | The week's topics belong to the subject, scope and level that the title and description indicate. |
| `PARTIALLY_ALIGNED` | Some topics belong; others fall outside that scope or level, or the connection is indirect. |
| `NOT_ALIGNED` | The week's topics belong to a different subject. |
| `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` | The content, as written, cannot be compared with the title. |

**Needs an institutional definition**

- How an orientation week (course requirements, objectives, grading and policies) should be judged against the course title is not defined by LCUP. Choose the judgment you can defend and explain your reading. Answer "yes" to "Requires an institutional decision?" if your judgment depends on it.

### 4.2 Course Content ↔ ILO

**Question.** Does this week's course content provide the subject matter that the ILO expects students to learn or demonstrate?

**Consider**

- what the ILO names, against the topics the week lists.
- the ILO's scope: if it names topics taught in other weeks, whether this week still supplies a meaningful part of it.
- whether the content lets students do what the ILO's verb asks (for example, apply, design or analyse what is listed).

| Judgment | When |
|---|---|
| `ALIGNED` | The content covers what the ILO names. |
| `PARTIALLY_ALIGNED` | The content covers part of what the ILO names, or the ILO reaches beyond this content in a way that matters. |
| `NOT_ALIGNED` | The ILO names subject matter that this content does not include. |
| `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` | The content or the ILO is too general or too incomplete to compare. |

**Needs an institutional definition**

- Some ILOs concern values formation (for example, "Demonstrate the Filipino-Catholic Augustinian Spirituality through value webbing") and appear in lessons on technical topics. How LCUP expects such an ILO to relate to the week's technical content is not defined. Explain your reading. Answer "yes" to "Requires an institutional decision?" if your judgment depends on it.

### 4.3 ILO ↔ CLO

**Question.** Does achieving this ILO contribute to the CLO it is mapped to?

**Consider**

- whether the ILO develops part of what the CLO states (its competency and subject). An ILO need not cover the whole CLO; several ILOs usually build one CLO.
- the CLO's required LCUP classification, which follows from its number: CLO 1 Introductory, CLO 2 Enabling, CLO 3 Demonstrating, CLO 4 Integration of Values. Consider whether the ILO's purpose and level suit that classification as you understand it. The classification belongs to the CLO, not to the week.
- each mapping on its own: an ILO mapped to two CLOs forms two relationships, and each is judged separately.
- the week's content, which shows what the ILO concerns in that lesson.

| Judgment | When |
|---|---|
| `ALIGNED` | The ILO develops a recognisable part of the CLO, in a way consistent with the CLO's classification as you understand it. |
| `PARTIALLY_ALIGNED` | The ILO develops part of the CLO, but an element that matters (for example the CLO's distinctive competency, or the fit with its classification) is missing or doubtful. |
| `NOT_ALIGNED` | The ILO develops nothing that the CLO states. |
| `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` | The statements are too general or too incomplete to decide. |

**Needs an institutional definition**

- The official definitions of the four classifications are not available to this project, and this rubric does not supply them. Do not assume one. If your judgment depends on what a classification means, write the meaning you applied in your justification. Answer "yes" to "Requires an institutional decision?" if your judgment depends on it.
- Whether technical ILOs may also be mapped to the values CLO (CLO 4, Integration of Values) is not defined.
- ILOs that are mapped to no CLO are listed separately by the research team and are not part of this review.

### 4.4 Course Content ↔ Learning Activities

**Question.** Do this week's learning activities engage students with the week's course content?

**Consider**

- whether the activities name, or clearly address, the topics the week lists.
- coverage: whether the activities reach the main topics or only some of them.

| Judgment | When |
|---|---|
| `ALIGNED` | The activities address the content's topics. |
| `PARTIALLY_ALIGNED` | The activities address some of the topics, or address them only indirectly. |
| `NOT_ALIGNED` | The activities address a different subject. |
| `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` | The activities, as written, cannot be compared with the content. |

**Needs an institutional definition**

- LCUP does not say whether an activity or assessment worded only in general terms (for example, a teaching method, or a quiz "on the topics discussed") is enough evidence. It can be read as saying too little to judge, or read together with the week's content. Choose the reading you can defend, say which one you used, and answer "yes" to "Requires an institutional decision?".

### 4.5 ILO ↔ Learning Activities

**Question.** Do the learning activities give students the opportunity to do what the ILO states?

**Consider**

- the ILO's verb (what students must be able to do, for example explain, apply or design) and its object.
- whether at least one activity lets students practise that verb on that object.
- whether the kind of activity suits the kind of outcome, for example whether students practise a skill the ILO names.

| Judgment | When |
|---|---|
| `ALIGNED` | At least one activity lets students do what the ILO states. |
| `PARTIALLY_ALIGNED` | The activities address the ILO's subject but not what students must do, or only part of it. |
| `NOT_ALIGNED` | No activity addresses the ILO's subject or what it asks students to do. |
| `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` | The statements are too general to compare. |

**Needs an institutional definition**

- What activity evidence LCUP expects for a values-formation ILO (value webbing) is not defined.
- LCUP does not say whether an activity or assessment worded only in general terms (for example, a teaching method, or a quiz "on the topics discussed") is enough evidence. It can be read as saying too little to judge, or read together with the week's content. Choose the reading you can defend, say which one you used, and answer "yes" to "Requires an institutional decision?".

### 4.6 Course Content ↔ Assessment

**Question.** Does the assessment measure students' learning of this week's course content?

**Consider**

- whether the assessment tasks name, or clearly target, the topics the week lists.
- coverage: whether the tasks assess the main topics or only some of them.

| Judgment | When |
|---|---|
| `ALIGNED` | The assessment targets the content's topics. |
| `PARTIALLY_ALIGNED` | The assessment targets some of the topics, or targets them only in part. |
| `NOT_ALIGNED` | The assessment targets a different subject. |
| `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` | The assessment, as written, cannot be compared with the content. |

**Needs an institutional definition**

- LCUP does not say whether an activity or assessment worded only in general terms (for example, a teaching method, or a quiz "on the topics discussed") is enough evidence. It can be read as saying too little to judge, or read together with the week's content. Choose the reading you can defend, say which one you used, and answer "yes" to "Requires an institutional decision?".

### 4.7 Learning Activities ↔ Assessment

**Question.** Does the assessment measure what the learning activities prepare students to do?

**Consider**

- the correspondence between what students practise in the activities and what the tasks ask of them.
- whether the tasks ask for something that no activity prepares students for.

| Judgment | When |
|---|---|
| `ALIGNED` | The tasks assess what the activities prepare students to do. |
| `PARTIALLY_ALIGNED` | The tasks assess part of it, or also ask for something the activities do not prepare. |
| `NOT_ALIGNED` | The tasks ask for something the activities do not prepare students for. |
| `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` | The activities or the tasks, as written, cannot be compared. |

**Needs an institutional definition**

- LCUP does not say whether an activity or assessment worded only in general terms (for example, a teaching method, or a quiz "on the topics discussed") is enough evidence. It can be read as saying too little to judge, or read together with the week's content. Choose the reading you can defend, say which one you used, and answer "yes" to "Requires an institutional decision?".
- Whether every activity must be assessed in the same week is not defined: an assessment may come in a later week, or the activity may be formative.

## Part 5. Recording your judgment

| Column | What to write |
|---|---|
| Faculty judgment | One of the four codes, exactly as listed. |
| Academic justification | Quote the words you relied on from both statements, in quotation marks and exactly as printed. Then explain, in one to three sentences, why they support your judgment. For ILO ↔ CLO, say how you read the CLO's classification if it mattered. |
| Recommended correction, if necessary | Needed when your judgment is PARTIALLY_ALIGNED or NOT_ALIGNED; optional for INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE. Name the field to change (course content, ILO, CLO mapping, learning activity or assessment) and propose wording. The fix may belong to a field other than the two compared; say so. Do not propose changes to the institution's Vision, Mission, EGAs or Program Outcomes, and keep to what alignment needs: the institution has not yet set limits on recommended corrections. Write "None" if no correction is needed. |
| Reviewer ID | The code the research team gave you, not your name. |
| Within your expertise? (optional) | Answer "no" or "uncertain" if the subject is outside your field. Your judgment is still recorded. |
| Requires an institutional decision? (optional) | Answer "yes" if your judgment depends on a definition or policy that LCUP has not provided (Part 6). |
| Notes (optional) | Anything else, including structural problems you noticed or text you think was transcribed wrongly. |

## Part 7. What not to do

- Do not use any AI tool, chatbot or syllabus checker while reviewing, and do not paste the case text into any online service.
- Do not discuss the cases with other reviewers or with the syllabus authors until you have returned your form.
- Do not edit the evidence. If you think a statement was transcribed wrongly, say so under Notes.
- Do not assume that a relationship is aligned because the syllabus was approved, or misaligned because it was chosen for review.
- Judge each relationship on its own. A week or an ILO that appears in two cases may receive different judgments.
