# Faculty academic validation package (lcup-faculty-validation-1)

Prepared 2026-10-29 for the capstone research team. It is not sent, published or committed.

**Status.** The materials are ready for review by the research team. They contain no faculty judgment, no adjudication and no AI result. Claude has not been run on these 21 cases, and no paid request was made to prepare them. Nothing in the AI Checker, the 47 deterministic rules, the seven academic relationships, the scoring, the passing mark of 79, the submission gates or the frozen evaluation evidence was changed.

**Before anything is distributed,** read section 4. Several institutional permissions are still unrecorded, and the most important one, Decision 14 on using the approved syllabi, controls whether these materials may leave this computer at all.

## 1. Purpose

The materials ask qualified faculty to judge 21 academic relationships taken from the three verified LCUP syllabi: IT 202 Computer Programming 1, IE-402 Systems Engineering and CpE 304 Fundamentals of Mixed Signals and Sensors. There are three cases for each of the seven relationships:

| Relationship | Cases |
|---|---|
| Course Title ↔ Course Content | FV-06, FV-08, FV-10 |
| Course Content ↔ ILO | FV-02, FV-14, FV-21 |
| ILO ↔ CLO | FV-04, FV-12, FV-17 |
| Course Content ↔ Learning Activities | FV-01, FV-11, FV-19 |
| ILO ↔ Learning Activities | FV-07, FV-16, FV-18 |
| Course Content ↔ Assessment | FV-05, FV-09, FV-20 |
| Learning Activities ↔ Assessment | FV-03, FV-13, FV-15 |

By course: IT 202 has FV-01 to FV-07 (7 cases), IE-402 has FV-08 to FV-16 (9) and CpE 304 has FV-17 to FV-21 (5).

The result is a **faculty reference**: an independent, adjudicated faculty judgment for each case. Only after that reference is frozen, and only if the project separately approves a run, can Claude's judgments on the same cases be compared with it.

The cases come from the frozen benchmark `lcup-academic-benchmark-1` (benchmark hash `ebd12920…bbf7`). Nothing was written or changed in them. The benchmark's labels are not faculty-approved ground truth: every genuine case has no expected judgment, and none is used here.

## 3. Blinding and confidentiality

- **Give reviewers only what is in `for-reviewers/`**: the booklet, the rubric and the form for their course. Never give them `coordinator-only/`, this README, the benchmark folders or any earlier evaluation report.
- **The selection record is not an answer key.** For each case it records a surface signal ("apparent alignment", "apparent partial alignment", "apparent misalignment" or "limited evidence"). Claude Code (an AI assistant) wrote these signals from the wording alone when it drew the sample, so that the sample would include varied cases. No faculty member has reviewed them. Never show them to reviewers, never put them in a prompt, and never score faculty or Claude against them.
- **Case IDs reveal nothing.** They follow course, week and relationship order.
- **No AI output is in the reviewer materials.** The three cases that already had a Claude output were left out of the sample, and the verifier checks that no AI output, benchmark ID or signal appears in `for-reviewers/`.
- **Minimal syllabus content.** The materials reproduce only what the judgments need: course identity, units and prerequisite, the course description, the CLOs, a one-line lesson outline and the selected week rows. They do not reproduce names, signatures, contact details, the core-values column, requirements, references or the grading system. Reviewers are identified by a code, not by name.
- **Keep everything on university computers.** Do not upload the materials to a cloud service or an AI tool, and do not commit them: the folder is untracked, and the repository is hosted on GitHub.

## 5. How to conduct the validation

**Step 1. Prepare the distribution.**
- Give each reviewer the form for their course: IT faculty `faculty-evaluation-form-IT202.xlsx`, IE faculty `-IE402.xlsx`, CpE faculty `-CpE304.xlsx`. Use the 21-case form only for a reviewer who is qualified in all three disciplines.
- Rename each copy with the reviewer's code (for example `faculty-evaluation-form-IT202-R-IT-01.xlsx`), and keep an unfilled copy.
- Give every reviewer the booklet (on screen or printed) and the rubric.
- The evidence cells of the form are protected against accidental change, with no password. The reviewer fills in only the yellow cells.

**Step 2. Calibrate (about 30 to 45 minutes, together, before anyone starts).**
- Walk through the rubric: the four judgments, the question for each relationship, and the difference between academic alignment and structural compliance (Part 3).
- Use only the fictional format example on the form's Instructions sheet, or other fictional examples. Do not discuss any of the 21 cases.
- Explain the two optional columns. "Within your expertise?" records confidence without discarding the judgment. "Requires an institutional decision?" flags a judgment that depends on a definition LCUP has not provided.
- Explain the CLO classification. CLO 1 is Introductory, CLO 2 Enabling, CLO 3 Demonstrating and CLO 4 Integration of Values, as LCUP requires. No official definitions are available, so a reviewer whose judgment depends on one writes the meaning they applied.

**Step 3. Review independently.**
- Each reviewer works alone, without AI tools and without consulting the syllabus authors or other reviewers.
- For each case they record the judgment, a justification that quotes both statements exactly, a recommended correction where the judgment is PARTIALLY_ALIGNED or NOT_ALIGNED, and their reviewer code.
- They return the form to the coordinator.

**Step 4. Check completeness.** The coordinator checks every row for:
- a judgment from the list;
- quotations from both statements in the justification;
- a correction where one is needed;
- the reviewer code.

Return a form only to complete missing entries, never to change a judgment. Keep the returned files unchanged, as received.

**Step 5. Measure reviewer agreement.**
- Copy `faculty-claude-comparison-template.xlsx` to a working file outside this folder.
- Enter each pair of reviewers' codes and judgments in columns F to I.
- The Summary sheet computes the share of complete pairs that agree, and Cohen's kappa over the two review slots. Read kappa descriptively: with 21 cases it is unstable, and different people fill the slots for different courses, so also report agreement per course.

**Step 6. Adjudicate.**
- For every case, the discipline adjudicator reads both justifications and records one faculty reference judgment.
- Never average the reviewers and never take an automatic majority. Whether agreement between both reviewers alone counts as a reference is a decision for the institution (I10).
- List other defensible judgments, and mark the case ambiguous, only when it is genuinely open to more than one reading (I9).
- Record:
  - the adjudicator's code, the date and the reason;
  - whether the judgment depends on an institutional decision;
  - an exact quotation from each side. The template names the side each quotation must come from, and the measurement accepts a quotation only if it is found, apart from spacing, in that statement.
- If INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE is the outcome, record it as the reference only if the institution accepts it as one (I8). Otherwise mark the case as pending.

**Step 7. Record and freeze the faculty reference.**
- Copy `coordinator-only/adjudication.template.json` to a working file outside this folder, and fill one entry per case:
  - `reviewStatus` "adjudicated", `adjudicatorId`, at least two distinct `reviewerIds`, `reviewedAt` (ISO date), `reason`;
  - `recordRef`, pointing to where the signed or returned forms are kept;
  - `acceptedJudgments`, with the reference first, and `ambiguous` (true or false);
  - `sourceEvidence` and `targetEvidence`.
- Set `provenance` to "faculty-adjudication" only when every entry is final.
- Check the file with the benchmark's own measurement, which makes no request of any kind. Before any Claude run, use the empty model template:

  `node scripts/lcup-academic-benchmark/measure.cjs --dataset docs/lcup-academic-benchmark/package/cases.json --adjudication <your adjudication file> --model docs/lcup-faculty-validation-1/coordinator-only/model-results.template.json`

  The status should read `awaiting_model_results`, and `facultyLabelled` should equal the number of adjudicated cases. Every case listed under `exclusions` has an incomplete entry, or a quotation that is not in the statement.
- Record the file's SHA-256 and date. From then on, the faculty reference does not change because of anything Claude says.

**Step 8. Compare with Claude: later, and only with approval. Not part of this milestone.**
- Required first:
  - Decision 14b, permission to send case text (without names or sign-off) to an external AI service;
  - the project owner's explicit approval of a pre-registered run, naming the scope (these 21 cases), model, prompt and contract versions, request count and cost cap;
  - the frozen faculty reference.
- The existing prepared bundles do not cover this sample:
  - `prepared-clo-classification-3` covers the 65 evaluable ILO ↔ CLO relationships, including FV-04, FV-12 and FV-17;
  - the other 18 cases have no prepared scope;
  - `prepared-final` and `prepared-r2` must be prepared again before any use.
- After an approved run:
  - enter Claude's status, judgment and recommended correction in columns V to AD of the working comparison file, and its run details in `model-results.template.json`;
  - run `measure.cjs` as above with the filled model file;
  - have faculty rate each Claude correction in column AE without revisiting their own judgments.
- `measure.cjs` is the authoritative computation; the workbook is a working view. Its false-approval and false-rejection columns use the same definitions as `measure.cjs`.

**Step 9. Report.**
- Report per relationship as counts: each relationship has only three cases, so percentages would mislead. Report reviewer agreement, and any later Claude agreement, as descriptive figures.
- List separately:
  - the cases flagged as depending on an institutional decision;
  - the ambiguous cases;
  - the cases left pending.
- State these limitations:
  - **This is a pilot of 21 cases.** It was deliberately varied: the cases were chosen to include apparent alignment, partial alignment and misalignment. It says nothing about how often the syllabi are aligned.
  - **The three syllabi were also used to design the evaluation prompts,** so any Claude comparison on them is a development-stage check, not an independent estimate of accuracy.
  - **The rubric is provisional,** and the CLO classification definitions are unavailable.
  - **No institutional acceptance threshold exists.**
  - **The AI Checker is advisory.** It never approves a syllabus; faculty decisions stand.
- Do not report the selection signals as results.

## 6. What the package does not cover

These are listed in full in `institutional-definitions-and-evidence-gaps.md` and `coordinator-only/missing-mappings-and-non-evaluable.md`:

- **26 relationships of the three syllabi cannot be evaluated:**
  - 15 ILOs mapped to no CLO;
  - 3 relationships of the CpE 304 Week 1 lesson, which has no ILO;
  - 8 CpE 304 Course Title ↔ Course Content cases held because of the course code's spelling.
- **3 CLOs are built by no ILO:** IT 202 CLO 4, and CpE 304 CLO 3 and CLO 4.
- **Coverage gaps in the sample:**
  - no authentic apparent misalignment for Course Title ↔ Course Content or for Course Content ↔ Learning Activities;
  - no apparent partial alignment for Course Content ↔ Assessment;
  - no CLO 2 (Enabling) case among the ILO ↔ CLO cases.

## 7. Replacing a case

Replace a case only before any reviewer has seen the package, for example when a reviewer turns out to be the syllabus author. The selection record lists two authentic reserves. Edit the selection in `build.cjs`, rebuild, verify, and note the change and the reason in the selection record.

## 8. What was not done

- No AI request of any kind was made: no Claude, Gemini or other provider, paid or free.
- No academic judgment was made. The selection signals are not judgments.
- No source, test, rule, configuration, benchmark or evaluation file was changed. The build only reads the benchmark package, after checking it against its manifest.
- Nothing was committed, pushed, deployed, published or sent.

## 9. Rebuilding and verifying

- `node scripts/lcup-faculty-validation/build.cjs` rebuilds the generated files. It works offline and gives byte-identical output. It refuses to write if any generated file has been changed since the last build, so a filled-in form in this folder is never overwritten. Fill in copies outside the folder anyway.
- `node scripts/lcup-faculty-validation/verify.cjs` (add `--verbose` to list every check) checks:
  - the manifest and that a rebuild gives identical files;
  - the blinding of `for-reviewers/` and the absence of personal details;
  - that every statement matches the benchmark text;
  - the case counts, and the form headers and validation;
  - that the templates load in `measure.cjs`;
  - that the benchmark package itself is unchanged.
